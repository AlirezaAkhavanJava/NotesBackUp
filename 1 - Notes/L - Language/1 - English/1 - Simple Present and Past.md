In English grammar, the **simple present** and **simple past** are two basic verb tenses. Here's what each one means and how it's used.

## Simple Present

**Definition:** The simple present expresses actions that happen regularly, facts that are generally true, habits, and states that exist now. It is also used for scheduled future events.

**Form:**
- Base verb for most subjects: *I/you/we/they work*
- Add **-s** or **-es** for third-person singular: *he/she/it works, goes, watches*

**Uses:**
1. **Habits/routines:** *She walks to school every day.*
2. **General facts:** *Water boils at 100°C.*
3. **Permanent situations:** *They live in London.*
4. **Scheduled events (future):** *The train leaves at 6 p.m.*
5. **Instructions/commentary:** *You mix the flour and eggs first.*

**Examples:**
- *I drink coffee every morning.*
- *He doesn't like spinach.*
- *Do you speak French?*

## Simple Past

**Definition:** The simple past expresses an action or state that began and finished at a definite time in the past. The time may be stated or understood.

**Form:**
- Regular verbs: add **-ed** → *worked, played, visited*
- Irregular verbs: special forms → *go → went, see → saw, eat → ate*
- Negative/questions use **did** + base verb: *didn't work, Did you go?*

**Uses:**
1. **Completed past actions:** *I visited Rome last year.*
2. **A sequence of past events:** *She woke up, showered, and left.*
3. **Past states/habits:** *He lived in Paris for ten years.*
4. **With definite past time expressions:** *yesterday, in 2010, two days ago, last week*

**Examples:**
- *They watched a movie last night.*
- *She didn't call me yesterday.*
- *Did you finish your homework?*

## Quick Comparison

| | Simple Present | Simple Past |
|---|---|---|
| **Time** | Now / always / regularly | Finished past |
| **Example** | *I work here.* | *I worked there last year.* |
| **Signal words** | every day, usually, often | yesterday, ago, last week, in 1999 |
| **Third person** | adds -s (*he works*) | same for all (*he worked*) |
| **Negatives** | don't/doesn't + base | didn't + base |

**Key contrast:** *I live in Tokyo* (simple present — true now) vs. *I lived in Tokyo* (simple past — no longer true, finished in the past).
