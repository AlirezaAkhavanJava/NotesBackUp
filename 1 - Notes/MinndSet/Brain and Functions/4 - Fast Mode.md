# Fast Mode Brain

*When your mind runs on quick hits instead of deep thought.*

---

## What it means

"Fast mode brain" is the state your mind slips into when it's been trained to expect constant, quick stimulation. Everything becomes a sprint — skim this, scroll that, react, move on. No settling, no depth, no sitting with a thought long enough for it to actually mean something.

It's not a diagnosis. It's a mode. And like any mode, you can get stuck in it without noticing.

---

## What it feels like from the inside

- You read something but your eyes just glide over the words
- You start a task and reach for your phone within seconds
- Silence or slowness feels almost physically itchy
- You're "busy" all day but can't name what you actually did
- Thinking hard feels like wading through water

The weird part: it feels *normal*. That's what makes it sneaky.

---

## Why the brain does it

Your brain is a prediction machine. It adapts to whatever you feed it.

Feed it fast → it optimizes for fast.
Feed it shallow → it gets good at shallow.

- Short-form content trains instant reward
- Switching tabs trains constant re-orienting
- Notifications train reflex over intention

Your brain isn't broken. It's just *very* good at becoming whatever you practice.

---

## Fast mode vs. slow mode

| Fast Mode | Slow Mode |
|---|---|
| Reacts | Reflects |
| Skims | Reads |
| Consumes | Digests |
| Constant switching | Sustained focus |
| Feels busy | Feels clear |

Neither is "bad" on its own. Fast mode has its place — quick decisions, emergencies, snapping back in a conversation. The problem is when it becomes your *default*.

> A brain stuck in fast mode can't do slow-mode work. And most of what matters — learning, creating, real thinking — is slow-mode work.

---

## How to shift out of it

You don't flip a switch. You practice the other mode until it comes back online.

- **Do one thing at a time** — even small things, deliberately
- **Let yourself be bored** — the itch is the withdrawal, not a problem
- **Read something long** — a real article, a chapter, slowly
- **Sit with a question** — don't Google it instantly, just think
- **Reduce inputs** — less feed, more space

The first few times feel awful. That's not failure. That's your brain recalibrating.

---

## What I'm taking from this

Fast mode isn't the enemy — being *stuck* in it is. The goal isn't to never think fast. It's to be able to choose slow when slow is what the moment needs.

My brain becomes whatever I practice. So I need to be careful what I practice.
