# Brain Rot

*The slow decay of a mind that's been fed too much nothing.*

---

## What it actually means

"Brain rot" started as internet slang but it's pointing at something real. It's the feeling that your attention span is shot, your thoughts are shallow, and your brain feels like mush after too long spent scrolling low-value content — short videos, rage bait, endless feeds, dopamine hits with zero substance.

It's not a medical diagnosis. But it describes a genuine pattern: **you consume a lot, and you retain almost nothing.**

---

## How it happens

The brain isn't lazy — it's efficient. It chases the path of least resistance.

- Short-form content gives instant reward with no effort
- The algorithm learns what keeps you hooked, not what helps you
- Boredom becomes unbearable because you've trained yourself to never sit with it
- Deep focus starts feeling *physically* uncomfortable

You don't notice it happening. You just wake up one day and realize you can't read a page without reaching for your phone.

---

## The signs

- You rewatch things you don't even enjoy
- You feel tired but not rested
- Long conversations or books feel like work
- You can't remember what you scrolled past five minutes ago
- Silence feels wrong

---

## Why it matters for self-improvement

Your mind is the tool you use for *everything* — your goals, your relationships, your growth. If it's running on junk fuel, everything downstream suffers.

> You can't build a sharp life on a dull mind.

The scary part isn't the brain rot itself. It's how *normal* it starts to feel.

---

## What actually helps

Not a dramatic digital detox. Small, honest corrections:

- **Boredom is a feature, not a bug** — let yourself be bored sometimes
- Swap scrolling for something slow: a walk, a real book, a long think
- Notice *why* you reach for the phone — usually it's avoiding something
- Protect one deep-focus block a day, even 20 minutes
- Cut the content that leaves you emptier than before

---

## What I'm taking from this

Brain rot isn't about hating the internet. It's about noticing when I've stopped *choosing* what goes into my head and started letting it be chosen for me.

The fix isn't discipline for its own sake. It's remembering that my attention is the one thing I can't buy back.
