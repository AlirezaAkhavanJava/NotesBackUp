
**AOL is an important part of the story, but it played a different role from Netscape and Internet Explorer.**

## AOL — America Online

AOL was primarily an **Internet service provider (ISP) and online service**, not a browser.

Its role was essentially:

> **AOL helped put millions of ordinary people online.**

### Think of the stack

          ↓

          ↓

   ┌──────┴──────┐
   │             │
Netscape         IE

          ↓

But AOL was more than just connectivity.

### 1. Before widespread Internet access

Getting online in the early/mid-1990s wasn't as trivial as it is today.

You might have needed:

- a modem
    
- a telephone line
    
- an ISP account
    
- networking configuration
    
- software
    

AOL packaged much of this into a relatively simple consumer service.

You essentially:

```text
Get AOL
   ↓
Install AOL software
   ↓
Dial modem
   ↓
Connect
   ↓
Use AOL's online services
   ↓
Eventually access the Internet/Web
```

That dramatically lowered the barrier for non-technical users.

---

## 2. AOL wasn't initially "the Web"

This distinction is important.

AOL originally provided its **own proprietary online environment**:

```text
                AOL
                 │
       ┌─────────┼──────────┐
       │         │          │
     Email     Chat       Forums
       │         │          │
       └─────────┼──────────┘
                 │
          AOL content/services
```

The **World Wide Web** was a separate system.

Eventually AOL increasingly connected its users to the broader Internet and Web.

So:

```text
AOL ≠ Web
AOL ≠ Internet
AOL = online service + Internet access/ISP
```

---

## 3. AOL and Netscape

This is where the history gets interesting.

In **1998, AOL acquired Netscape**.

So:

```text
Mosaic
   ↓
Netscape Navigator
   ↓
Netscape Communications
   ↓
       AOL acquires Netscape
              ↓
             AOL
```

This happened during the height of the **Microsoft vs. Netscape browser war**.

AOL was therefore not just an Internet-access company; through Netscape it became connected directly to the browser/platform battle.

---

## 4. AOL's biggest historical contribution

If we simplify the 1990s:

```text
Mosaic
   ↓
Made the Web visually accessible

Netscape
   ↓
Made Web browsing commercially popular

AOL
   ↓
Made getting online accessible to millions
```

And Microsoft:

```text
Internet Explorer
   ↓
Used Windows' enormous distribution
   ↓
Competed aggressively with Netscape
```

So AOL belongs in the history as one of the companies that helped transform the Internet from something used mainly by **technical/academic users** into something ordinary households could access.

---


AOL was **one of the major early consumer online services/ISPs**, but it was **not the first ISP**.

### Why?

The concept of an ISP emerged before AOL became a major consumer service.

A rough timeline is:

```text
1960s–70s
ARPANET
   ↓
1980s
Commercial/independent Internet access begins emerging
   ↓
1989
The World becomes one of the first commercial ISPs
   ↓
1990s
AOL becomes a MASSIVE consumer Internet service
   ↓
1990s
Internet access becomes mainstream
```

**The World (world.std.com)** is commonly cited as the **first commercial ISP**, beginning service in **1989**.

AOL itself started earlier, in **1985**, but it began primarily as a **proprietary online service**, not as the general Internet-access ISP we mean today.

So the precise statement is:

> **AOL was not the first ISP; it was one of the most influential early consumer online services and later became a major ISP, helping bring Internet access to millions of households.**

That's an important distinction because **"online service" and "ISP" weren't necessarily the same thing in the early 1980s/90s.**
