**Memory** is the brain's capacity to encode, store, and retrieve information — turning experiences into a form that can be preserved over time and recalled later.

## The Three-Stage Process

1. **Encoding** – Converting sensory input (what you see, hear, feel) into a neural code the brain can process. Attention plays a huge role here — you generally can't remember what you never really noticed.
2. **Storage** – Maintaining that encoded information over time, ranging from fractions of a second to a lifetime.
3. **Retrieval** – Accessing stored information when needed, whether consciously ("What was her name again?") or automatically (riding a bike without thinking about it).

## Types of Memory

**By duration:**

- **Sensory memory** – Lasts less than a second to a few seconds; a brief snapshot of raw sensory input (e.g., the "afterimage" you see when someone waves a sparkler).
- **Short-term/working memory** – Holds a small amount of information (roughly 4–7 items) for about 15–30 seconds, used for active tasks like mental math or holding a phone number before dialing.
- **Long-term memory** – Can last for years or a lifetime, with much larger (effectively unlimited) capacity.

**Within long-term memory:**

- **Explicit (declarative) memory** – Facts and events you can consciously recall.
    - _Episodic_: personal experiences ("my graduation day")
    - _Semantic_: general knowledge ("Paris is the capital of France")
- **Implicit (non-declarative) memory** – Skills and conditioned responses you don't consciously think about.
    - _Procedural_: motor skills (typing, driving)
    - _Priming and conditioning_: subtle influences from past experience



## Key Brain Structures Involved

- **Hippocampus** – Critical for forming new explicit memories and consolidating them into long-term storage; damage here can prevent new memories from forming (as in the famous case of patient H.M.).
- **Amygdala** – Adds emotional weight to memories, which is why emotionally charged events tend to be remembered more vividly.
- **Prefrontal cortex** – Involved in working memory and retrieval strategies.
- **Cerebellum and basal ganglia** – Support procedural/motor memory.
- **Cortex (distributed)** – Long-term memories are ultimately stored across widespread cortical networks, not in one single location.
