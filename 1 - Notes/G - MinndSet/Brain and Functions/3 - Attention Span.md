# Attention Spans

*The most valuable thing you own, and the easiest thing to give away.*

---

## The core idea

Attention isn't just "focus." It's the raw material of everything you do — learning, creating, connecting, thinking. Where your attention goes, your life goes. Literally.

The problem is we treat it like it's infinite. It's not. It's a finite resource that gets spent whether you're aware of it or not.

---

## What's actually happening

Your attention span isn't fixed. It's trainable — in *both* directions.

- Spend hours on short-form content → your brain adapts to expect constant novelty
- Sit with one hard thing for a while → your brain relearns how to stay

The frustrating part: **it's way easier to shorten it than to lengthen it.** One week of scrolling can undo months of reading. That asymmetry matters.

---

## The myth of "I just have a short attention span"

Most people aren't born this way. They *trained* it short, usually without realizing.

And here's the thing — a short attention span isn't a personality trait. It's a habit wearing a costume.

> You don't have a short attention span. You have an untrained one.

That reframe matters, because traits feel permanent and habits don't.

---

## Why it's getting worse

- Every app is engineered to fragment your focus
- Notifications turn attention into a reflex, not a choice
- Multitasking *feels* productive but shreds deep focus
- The brain gets rewarded for switching, so it switches more

We're not weak. We're just playing against systems built by very smart people to win.

---

## What actually helps

Not willpower. Environment and small reps.

- **Single-task** — one thing at a time, even boring things
- **Boredom reps** — waiting in line without pulling out your phone is training
- **Longer inputs** — books, podcasts, essays instead of clips
- **One focus block a day** — start small, protect it like it matters (because it does)
- **Friction** — make the distracting thing harder to reach than the good thing

The goal isn't monk-like focus. It's *choice* — being able to direct your attention instead of it being yanked around.

---

## What I'm taking from this

Attention is the front door to my whole life. If I don't guard it, something else will decide what walks in.

I don't need to be perfect. I just need to notice when I've stopped choosing — and start choosing again.
