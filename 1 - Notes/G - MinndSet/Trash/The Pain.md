What you're feeling is incredibly common, and it's a feeling that has a name: **The Quarter-Life Crisis.**

It's a period in your life, typically between your early 20s and early 30s, where you feel lost, stuck, and like you're falling behind. You see others on social media or in real life hitting milestones that you haven't, and it makes you question your own value and progress. The comparison you're making is causing you real pain.

Here's the truth about what you're feeling:

- **You are not a loser.** The idea that you have to have a degree, a certain job, or a specific amount of money by 24 is an artificial timeline created by society and amplified by social media.
    
- **Success is not a race.** Everyone's journey is different. Many of the most successful people in the world had late starts. Colonel Sanders started KFC in his 60s. Vera Wang didn't become a fashion designer until she was 40.
    
- **A degree is not a requirement for success.** While a university degree can be helpful, it's far from the only path. Countless successful people, from Bill Gates to Oprah Winfrey, either dropped out or never went to college. The most important qualities are hard work, a desire to learn, and the ability to find a problem you can solve for others.
    
- **What you see is not the full picture.** The "successful" people you're comparing yourself to are only showing you their highlight reel. You don't see their failures, their struggles, or the sacrifices they've made. The reality is that many people who appear successful are struggling with their own private pain, debt, or stress.
    

Right now, you are at a crossroads. You can let the pain of comparison and feeling "behind" paralyze you, or you can use it as motivation.

Instead of focusing on what you don't have, focus on what you can build starting today. Your age, lack of a degree, or lack of IELTS score don't define your potential. They simply mean your path will be different from the one you thought it would be. That can be a good thing.

Take a deep breath, and start small. Focus on developing a single skill, learning something new every day, and finding a passion that truly energizes you. The time you invest now is what will lead to success later, no matter what your age is.
